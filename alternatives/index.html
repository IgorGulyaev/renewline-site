One question decides most of it: do you run an RMM? Almost every lifecycle tool sources its data from an agent installed on managed endpoints. If you have that agent, those tools do things we do not. If you sell licences and manage no endpoints, they have nothing to read, and the shortlist collapses to a much smaller set.

The options

What each kind is actually good at.

Category leader

Lifecycle managers (ScalePad Lifecycle Manager and similar)

The most complete answer if you are an MSP. Warranty data is looked up automatically, assets age themselves, and the client-facing reporting is mature.

  • Hardware warranty automation nobody else matches
  • Built-in QBR and budget reporting
  • Needs an RMM or PSA integration to have any data at all
  • Reported at roughly $149–250+ per month and scaling with size, on twelve-month auto-renewing terms, with cancellation handled through sales rather than a billing page
  • Reported to treat firewall and security-licence expiry as manual-entry fields rather than automated data
Cheaper cohort

Dedicated renewal and vCIO trackers

A group of smaller tools has grown up positioning explicitly against the leader's price and contract terms — reported price points run from about $29 to $375 per month depending on the product and client count.

  • Much cheaper, often month to month
  • Usually quicker to set up
  • Most still assume an MSP shape: clients, managed devices, QBRs
  • Feature depth varies a lot — check whether renewals are the product or a tab
Already paid for

Your PSA's contract module

  • No extra cost, no extra login, no sync
  • Fine when the PSA is genuinely your system of record
  • Only as good as how current you keep it, which is the usual failure
  • Tracks dates; rarely produces the renewal quote
Adjacent

Generic expiry-reminder and contract tools

  • Cheap, simple, work for any kind of expiring thing
  • Good if you also track insurance, certifications, leases
  • No concept of vendor, SKU, seat count, margin or uplift
  • You get a reminder, then still build the quote by hand
The incumbent nobody lists

A spreadsheet

Most small resellers use one, and for small numbers it is the right answer. It stops working when nobody opens it in a busy month. If you are going to use a sheet, use a good one: our template is free and colours a renewal red once it has passed.

  • Free, flexible, already understood by everyone
  • Silent: it never tells you anything, you have to go and look
  • The renewal you miss is the one nobody opened the sheet for
Where we fit

Renewline, and when not to use it.

We built for the case none of the above covers: a reseller whose renewal data is in a spreadsheet and who has no agent anywhere.
